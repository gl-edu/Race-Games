Original (Explicación, as printed)

El juego se compone de dos partes, y se juega entre dos personas. Cada jugador tiene diez y seis piezas y dos comandantes; las piezas se colocan sobre las lineas 1 y 2 del tablero; los comandantes donde se quiera de las lineas 3 y 4. Los comandantes no pueden salir de su territorio; no hacen más que un paso á la vez; andan de todos lados y en cualquiera línea; tienen el derecho de tomar las piezas, haciéndose como en el juego de damas, siempre que haya un lugar libre detras de la pieza; pasan encima de la pieza, y se coloca en el lugar libre, pudiendo tomar otras piezas á la vez, si se encuentran en las mismas condiciones. Es obligación tomar las piezas, y en caso de olvido, el adversario debe advertirlo, si es de su interés que le tomen la pieza. Las piezas solo van en las líneas coloradas, á derecha ó á izquierda, pero no pueden ir para atras. Las piezas que lleguen á la línea número 1 del otro campo, ya no pueden ser tomadas. La partida está terminada, cuando uno de los jugadores ha metido la última pieza en el campo enemigo. En caso de que los dos lo hagan al mismo tiempo, el que ha metido más piezas ganará la partida.

English translation

The game has two sides, and is played between two people. Each player has sixteen pieces and two commanders. Pieces are placed on rows 1 and 2 of the board; commanders anywhere on rows 3 and 4. Commanders may not leave their own territory. They move only one step at a time, in any direction along any line. They may capture pieces as in checkers, provided there is a free space behind the piece: they jump over it and land in the free space, and may capture several pieces in the same turn if the same condition holds each time. Capturing is mandatory — if a player forgets, the opponent must point it out when it's in their interest to have the piece taken. Pieces move only along the red lines, to the right or left, but never backward. A piece that reaches row 1 on the opponent's side can no longer be captured. The game ends when a player has gotten their last piece into enemy territory. If both do so at the same time, whoever got more pieces across wins.

How this build resolves the game's ambiguities

  • Who can capture: the original text describes capturing only in the commanders’ paragraph. This build follows your call to treat it as standard checkers — regular pieces capture too, forward‑diagonally only (matching their movement restriction), while commanders capture one step in any of the 8 directions.
  • Commander territory: read literally — each side’s commanders can never leave their own four rows, so they only ever fight enemy pieces that have advanced into their territory.
  • Win condition: interpreted as every one of a player’s surviving 16 pieces reaching their opponent’s row 1 (matching the immunity milestone described just before it). If both sides complete this on consecutive turns, whoever has more pieces home wins; equal counts are a draw.
  • Attrition rule (not in the original text): if a player's last regular piece is captured, they can no longer fulfil the win condition, so the game ends immediately in the opponent's favor.
  • Commanders don't count toward the "pieces home" tally — only the original 16 rank‑and‑file pieces do, matching the text's specific word piezas.
  • Immunity is real: a piece that reached its safe row can't be captured by any means, including a commander jumping it sideways along that row.
  • No legal moves (not in the original text): in long games a side can end up with every piece boxed in. Following standard draughts convention, a player with no legal move on their turn loses immediately rather than the game stalling.
